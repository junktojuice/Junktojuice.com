Valuation
What actually moves the number

If you want a rough sense of where your lot will land before you reach out, these are the things we look at first.

Generation, more than age

A five-year-old server from a widely deployed generation can be worth more than a three-year-old orphan platform. What matters is whether anyone still runs it.

Completeness

Caddies, rails, power supplies, licenses and original modules meaningfully raise value. A stripped chassis is worth a fraction of a complete one.

Volume and uniformity

Forty identical laptops are easier to move than forty different ones, and the offer reflects that. Matched lots price better.

Documentation

Known provenance, service history and clean asset records reduce risk on our end, which lets us pay more on yours.

Working vs. untested

We buy both. Confirmed-working units carry a premium, but untested and even faulty equipment still has component value worth quoting.

Timing

Secondary market pricing moves. Equipment sitting in storage for three more years will almost always be worth less than it is today.

One thing worth knowing: the single most common way businesses lose money here is by holding equipment "until we have time to deal with it." Depreciation on the secondary market is steep and continuous. If a refresh is coming, the time to get a quote is when the new gear is ordered, not a year after the old gear is unplugged.

The process
From asset list to payment
1

Send us what you have

An asset list, a spreadsheet export, or photos. Model and serial info helps us price accurately, but we'll work with whatever you've got — and for large, mixed or hard-to-describe lots, just ask and we'll come walk it with you in person.

2

We make an offer

Usually within one business day, itemized by category so you can see the reasoning and pass it up the chain internally.

3

We collect and document

Serialized inventory is captured on site before loading. You keep a signed manifest of everything that left the building.

4

Data destroyed, you get paid

Sanitization or physical destruction to your spec, certificates issued, and payment by check or ACH.

When we'll say no. We'd rather be upfront than waste your time: we sometimes decline lots. Usually that's a load with essentially no recoverable value, a job where travel and labor exceed anything we could recover, material we're not equipped to handle safely, or equipment we can't establish clear ownership of. If that's your situation we'll tell you quickly and, where we can, point you toward someone better suited. A fast no is more useful to you than a slow maybe.

On larger or mixed lots we can quote two ways: a firm number up front, or a settlement after everything is tested and graded. The firm offer is simpler and faster. The settlement usually nets more if the equipment turns out to be in better shape than the photos suggested. We'll tell you honestly which one we think fits your lot.
