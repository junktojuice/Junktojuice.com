Standards
What we mean by "sanitized"
NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 1 is the current federal guideline for media sanitization and it's what we work to. It's method-appropriate rather than one-size-fits-all: modern SSDs and self-encrypting drives are handled with cryptographic erase or firmware-level sanitize commands, while traditional magnetic drives get a verified overwrite. This matters, because multi-pass overwriting — the old DoD 5220.22-M approach — is both unnecessary on modern magnetic media and unreliable on flash, where wear-levelling can leave data in cells the overwrite never touches. Every sanitization is verified, and any drive that fails verification is physically destroyed instead of resold. No exceptions.
Physical destruction applies to storage media specifically — hard drives, SSDs, flash chips, and the storage inside phones and tablets. The drive is shredded or drilled so data is unrecoverable by any means. The rest of the device is unaffected and can still be resold or recycled normally; we're not destroying the whole laptop, only the part that holds data.
Devices with storage we can't reach — printers with internal drives, multifunction copiers, VoIP phones, network appliances with configuration and credentials in flash, badge and access control systems — are treated as data-bearing and handled the same way. These are the ones most often missed in a decommission, and they're a common source of unpleasant surprises.
