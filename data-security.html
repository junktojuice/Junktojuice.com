Data security

Your data doesn't leave with your hardware.

Every device that stores data is wiped or physically destroyed before anything else happens to it. You get a certificate of destruction for your records.

Wiped

Drives are erased following NIST SP 800-88, the federal guideline for media sanitization. SSDs get a firmware-level secure erase, not just a format.

Or destroyed

Prefer physical destruction? Drives are drilled or crushed so the data can't be recovered. Just say so when you book.

Documented

A certificate of destruction lists what was collected and how each drive was handled.

The devices people forget

It's not just computers. Copiers and printers with internal drives, VoIP phones, network equipment with saved passwords, and phones and tablets all hold data. We treat every one of them the same way.

Should I wipe things first?

You don't need to. A factory reset often isn't a real wipe, so we treat every device as if it still holds data. If your own policy says to wipe or pull drives first, go ahead; it won't get in the way.

Want to keep the drives?

That's fine too. Pull them and hang on to them, and we'll take the rest.

Have a specific requirement?

Tell us the standard or policy you're working to and we'll tell you plainly whether we can meet it.
